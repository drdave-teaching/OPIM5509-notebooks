Module 1 — Notebooks

Everything in Module 1 runs in Google Colab. Click a link, then Runtime → Run all. There is nothing to download and no Google Drive to mount — the data loads from stable public sources, so a notebook that works for me works for you.

Before you type anything: do File → Save a copy in Drive. The GitHub version is read-only for you, and unsaved edits vanish when the tab closes. Your copy lands in My Drive / Colab Notebooks / — always that folder, no matter where you opened it from.

Work through these in order

  1. 1. Welcome & Setup
    Colab, GitHub, checking your library versions, and reading a .shape. Start here even if you feel confident — it is five minutes and it prevents most week-one problems.

  2. 2. California Housing — EDA
    What a row actually represents, and two genuine problems hiding in the data: a target that was capped at $500,001, and per-household averages that cannot possibly be true. Also multicollinearity, and a map of California drawn from two ordinary numeric columns.

  3. 3. All the Models — Regression
    The modeling skeleton, data leakage, five models fit with identical surrounding code, MAE vs RMSE vs R², and overfitting made visible as a train/test gap.

  4. 4. All the Models — Classification
    The same skeleton asked a yes/no question. Confusion matrix in plain business language, precision vs recall, and the idea that carries the whole semester: predict() is just predict_proba() compared against a threshold you chose.

  5. 5. General EDA Template
    A blank worksheet. Fill it in from memory, and type the code rather than pasting it. Keep a copy — this is the page to open when the final project lands.

Optional appendix

ROC, AUC, and the Decision Threshold — no video, entirely optional.

If the ROC curve in notebook 4 felt like a plot that appeared out of nowhere, this builds one by hand from twelve loan applicants you can check with a pencil. You watch the false positive rate move as the threshold slides, compute AUC two different ways, and see why the threshold is a business decision rather than a technical one.

The one picture to take out of Module 1

read data  ->  split  ->  scale on train  ->  [ MODEL ]  ->  fit / predict  ->  evaluate

Only the bracketed step changes for the rest of the semester. In Module 2 it becomes a dense neural network and nothing else moves. That is why we spend week one here.
